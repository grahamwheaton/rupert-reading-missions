The Stuck Summit

FICTION

Tom and Rupert were heading up a rocky mountain track with Dad.

The old green 4x4 crawled over stones and deep ruts.

Scout the black Labrador sat in the back.

His nose was pressed against the window.

“Look!” said Rupert.

A white 4x4 was stuck ahead.

Its back wheels were buried in thick mud.

The driver tried to move.

The tyres spun, but the vehicle stayed still.

Dad stopped on firm ground.

“First we check that everyone is safe,” he said.

Tom and Rupert stayed well away from the vehicles.

The driver climbed out.

“I was trying to reach the old lookout,” he said.

“I’m taking photographs of eagles for the ranger.”

Dad opened the back of their 4x4.

Inside was a bright recovery strap.

It was thick and very strong.

Dad checked the proper recovery points on both vehicles.

He explained that a strap must never be fixed to a weak part.

Everyone stood well clear.

Dad drove forward very slowly.

The strap tightened.

The stuck 4x4 moved an inch.

Then another.

Its tyres found traction.

Traction means the tyres can grip the ground instead of sliding.

“It’s moving!” shouted Rupert.

With one final pull, the white 4x4 climbed out of the rut.

The driver gave them a huge grin.

“Thank you. Want to come to the lookout with me?”

The two vehicles continued up the mountain.

They did not race.

On rough ground, slow and careful was better.

Near the top, the track became too rocky for the vehicles.

They parked and walked the final part.

Scout bounded ahead.

Tom carried the camera bag.

Rupert spotted a stone tower on the ridge.

The old lookout had been built many years ago.

Mountain rescue volunteers once used it to watch for people who needed help.

Then the photographer pointed into the sky.

“There!”

A huge bird circled above the valley.

Its wings hardly moved.

“An eagle?” whispered Rupert.

The photographer nodded and lifted his camera.

Click.

Rupert looked down at the tiny tracks far below.

One muddy rut had stopped a 4x4.

But helping its driver had led them all the way here.

Tom smiled.

“Good job we stopped.”

DID YOU KNOW?

Off road drivers often lower their speed on rough ground. Going slowly gives the driver more time to choose a safe line and helps the tyres keep their grip.
