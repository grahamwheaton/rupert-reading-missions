The Broken Bridge

FICTION

Tom, Dad and Scout were following an old mountain road.

Rain had hammered the valley during the night.

Now the river below them was fast and noisy.

Their 4x4 bumped over stones and shallow puddles.

Scout watched from the back seat.

Then Dad stopped.

“That does not look right.”

A wooden bridge crossed the river ahead.

One side had dropped.

Several thick planks were broken.

And there was a truck on it.

Its front wheels were on the bridge.

Its back wheels were still on the track.

The driver stood safely on the bank.

“The bridge moved under me,” he said. “I stopped straight away.”

Tom looked at the truck.

“Can we pull it backwards?”

Dad nodded.

“Perhaps. But first we make a safe plan.”

They parked their 4x4 on solid ground.

Dad took out the winch cable.

A winch is a machine that winds in a strong cable.

It can pull something heavy without needing to drive towards it.

Dad checked the truck's proper recovery point.

Then he looked for somewhere strong to hold their own vehicle.

A huge pine tree stood beside the track.

“That can be our anchor,” Dad said.

An anchor is something strong that helps stop another thing from moving.

They used a wide tree strap around the trunk.

Tom and the driver moved well away from the cable.

Scout stayed safely inside the 4x4.

Dad began to winch.

CLICK. WHIRR. CLICK.

The cable tightened.

The truck moved backwards by a few centimetres.

A plank creaked.

Dad stopped.

Everyone checked the bridge.

Then they continued.

WHIRR.

The front tyres rolled back.

One tyre reached the dirt.

Then the other.

The truck was off the bridge.

“Yes!” said Tom.

But their journey was not over.

They could not use the damaged bridge.

Dad unfolded the paper map.

There was another crossing several miles upstream.

It meant a longer drive.

Tom grinned.

“More trail?”

Dad laughed.

“More trail.”

They turned the 4x4 around.

Scout's tail thumped against the seat.

The shortcut was closed.

But sometimes the long way round makes the better adventure.

Recovery fact:

People should stay well away from a winch cable while it is under load. Recovery equipment stores a lot of force, so careful planning and the correct attachment points matter.

Questions

1. Why did the truck driver stop on the bridge?

2. What did the pine tree do during the recovery?

3. Would you be disappointed by the longer route, or excited? Why?
