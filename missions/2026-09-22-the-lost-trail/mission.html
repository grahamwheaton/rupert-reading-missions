The Lost Trail

FICTION — Mountain Bike Mission

Tom stopped his mountain bike at the top of the hill.

Two trails went into the woods.

One had a blue sign. The other was narrow, muddy and had no sign at all.

“Blue trail,” said Tom. “That’s our route.”

His friend Sam checked the map. Their job was simple: ride to the old forest hut, meet Dad there, and be back before lunch.

They rolled down the trail.

CRUNCH. CRUNCH.

Their tyres gripped the loose stones. Tom stood up on the pedals as the track became bumpy.

Then the trail dipped into the trees.

It was darker here. Ferns grew beside the track and wet roots crossed it like ropes.

“Roots!” called Tom.

He slowed down and let the bike roll over them.

Sam followed.

Then they reached a fork in the trail.

There was a post, but the blue arrow was gone.

“Which way?” asked Sam.

Tom looked at the ground.

The left trail was wide. The right trail climbed steeply uphill.

“The map says we should cross a stream,” Tom said. “I can hear water to the left.”

They took the left trail.

Soon they found the stream.

A little wooden bridge crossed it.

“Correct route!” said Sam.

But just beyond the bridge, they found trouble.

A tree had fallen across the trail.

They could not ride over it.

Tom got off his bike.

“We don’t need to move the tree,” he said. “We can carry the bikes around it.”

They lifted their bikes and carefully walked around the end of the trunk.

Then they carried on.

The trail began to climb.

Tom changed into an easier gear.

His legs kept turning.

Pedal. Pedal. Pedal.

At last, the trees opened.

There was the old forest hut.

Dad was waiting outside.

“I wondered where you were,” he said.

“The sign was missing and a tree blocked the trail,” said Tom.

Dad smiled. “So how did you find the way?”

Tom tapped the map in his pocket.

“We didn’t guess.”

Trail fact: Mountain-bike trails often use colours to show difficulty. In the UK, blue trails are usually moderate routes. Red and black trails are harder and need more skill.

Mission Questions

1. What clue helped Tom choose the correct trail at the fork?

2. Why did Tom change into an easier gear on the hill?

3. What would you pack for a ride through the forest?
