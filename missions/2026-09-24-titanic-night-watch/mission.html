Titanic: The Night Watch

FACT

On 10 April 1912, a huge ship left Southampton.

Her name was Titanic.

She was almost 270 metres long.

That is longer than two football pitches.

Titanic was a passenger liner.

She carried rich travellers, families starting new lives, and hundreds of crew.

Deep inside the ship, men fed coal into enormous boilers.

The steam powered the engines.

High above them were four tall funnels.

But only three carried smoke.

The fourth helped with air flow and made the ship look even grander.

For several days, Titanic steamed west across the Atlantic Ocean.

The sea became colder.

Other ships sent warnings about ice.

On the night of 14 April, two lookouts stood high in the crow's nest.

It was dark and very calm.

At about 11:40 at night, one lookout saw something ahead.

An iceberg.

He rang the warning bell three times.

The crew tried to turn the ship.

Titanic nearly missed the ice.

But the iceberg scraped along her right side.

Water began entering several compartments.

A compartment is a separate section inside a ship.

Titanic had watertight doors between many of them.

The doors could close to slow flooding.

But too many compartments had been damaged.

The bow sank lower into the sea.

The crew sent wireless distress messages.

They also prepared the lifeboats.

There were not enough lifeboat places for everyone aboard.

Some early boats even left with empty seats.

Just over two and a half hours after hitting the iceberg, Titanic disappeared beneath the Atlantic.

More than 1,500 people died.

About 700 survived.

Ships hurried to help.

The Carpathia arrived after the sinking and rescued survivors from the lifeboats.

The disaster changed safety rules at sea.

Ships began carrying enough lifeboat space for everyone.

Radio watches improved too.

In 1985, explorers finally found Titanic's wreck.

It lies about 3,800 metres below the surface.

That is nearly four kilometres down.

DID YOU KNOW?

An iceberg can hide most of its ice below the water. The small part above the sea can make the whole iceberg look much smaller than it really is.
